## 1. Summary of Current Project Plans

### 1.1 Project Overview and Core Architecture (From Proposal)

Our objective is to safely fine-tune an imitation-learned autonomous driving policy on safety-critical failure scenarios using a **Model-Based Reinforcement Learning (MBRL)** framework, **GATE-SAC** (*Gated Adaptive Truncation and Ensemble Soft Actor-Critic*). Autonomous driving pipelines commonly rely on closed-loop reinforcement learning (RL) to repair behavioral failures in imitation-learned policies, yet standard model-free RL is sample-inefficient and lacks explicit safety guarantees. GATE-SAC addresses both limitations by integrating four mechanisms inside the MetaDrive driving simulator:

1. **Imitation Base (Behavior Cloning):** A base driving policy will be pretrained via supervised Behavior Cloning (BC) on expert driving demonstrations so that the agent acquires fundamental lane-keeping and speed-control skills prior to closed-loop RL exploration.
2. **State-Space World-Model Ensemble:** An ensemble of four Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) will be trained on a real-environment replay buffer to predict one-step transition dynamics—next state, reward, and episode termination—directly in the continuous vector observation space (ego kinematics, navigation checkpoints, and a 1-D LiDAR distance array), with continuous control actions $a = (a_{\text{steer}}, a_{\text{throttle}}) \in [-1, 1]^2$.
3. **Uncertainty-Gated Imagination and Pessimistic Reward Penalty:** To reduce reliance on costly simulator interactions, the policy will train on short branched rollouts generated inside the learned ensemble. An **uncertainty gate** will dynamically truncate an imagined rollout whenever prediction disagreement across the four ensemble members exceeds a threshold, preventing compounding model hallucinations from corrupting value learning. Because a purely hard cutoff can discard informative states immediately preceding rare collisions or leave residual model error unpenalized, imagined transitions will also incorporate a MOPO-style pessimistic reward penalty,
   $$\tilde{r}(s, a) = \hat{r}(s, a) - \lambda \, u(s, a),$$
   where $u(s, a)$ denotes ensemble disagreement variance and $\lambda \ge 0$ is a risk-sensitivity coefficient.
4. **Constrained Off-Policy Optimization (Lagrangian SAC):** A Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) agent will train off-policy on hybrid mini-batches mixing real and imagined transitions, augmented with an independent **Lagrangian cost critic** and adaptive dual multiplier to enforce a strict safety budget specified in collisions per kilometer.

Our primary benchmark will be **Fixed-Horizon MBPO** trained with the exact same four-model dynamics ensemble, directly testing whether uncertainty-adaptive gating and reward pessimism prevent model exploitation and unsafe driving behaviors. As a stretch goal, we plan to explore upgrading the state representation to camera images paired with a latent Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM).

### 1.2 Planned Refinements Since the Proposal (Feedback Requested)

Based on a deeper technical analysis of the related literature surveyed in Section 2, we plan several refinements to our experimental and algorithmic design. **All changes relative to the original proposal are highlighted in bold below, and we welcome feedback on each point:**

1. **Expert Demonstration Source and Failure-Scenario Definition:** Real-world motion datasets such as the Waymo Open Motion Dataset record spatial trajectories rather than steering and throttle commands, requiring an auxiliary inverse-dynamics model before Behavior Cloning can be applied, and log-replayed surrounding traffic is non-reactive by default. **For our core experiments, we plan to collect expert demonstrations using MetaDrive's built-in expert policy inside `SafeMetaDriveEnv` (which includes static obstacles, dynamic reactive traffic, and native binary safety costs), identify the specific scenario seeds on which the pretrained BC policy fails, and target RL fine-tuning to a mixture of those failure scenarios and nominal scenarios. Evaluation on imported Waymo/ScenarioNet logs will be treated as an evaluation stretch goal.**
2. **Imitation-to-RL Initialization Protocol:** Our original proposal mentioned both fine-tuning an imitation policy and training a randomly initialized SAC agent. Prior work on offline-to-online fine-tuning (e.g., AWAC, DAPG, and BC-SAC) demonstrates that pairing a pretrained BC actor with randomly initialized critics causes severe initial policy degradation ("actor unlearning") due to inaccurate early critic gradients. **We therefore plan to initialize the SAC actor directly from the pretrained BC weights, seed the real replay buffer with expert transitions, freeze the actor during an initial critic warm-up phase while the reward and cost critics converge, and include an annealed BC regularization term $\beta \lVert \mu_\phi(s) - a^* \rVert^2$ (with $\beta \to 0$) in the actor objective.**
3. **Strict Decoupling of Reward and Safety Cost:** Including $-\lambda c_{\text{collision}}$ inside the scalar environment reward *and* training a separate Lagrangian cost critic double-counts collision penalties and distorts the safety budget. **Following standard Constrained MDP formulations, we plan to keep the environment reward purely task-directed ($r = r_{\text{progress}} - r_{\text{lane}}$) and route all collision and road-departure events exclusively through a binary cost signal $c_t \in \{0, 1\}$, the dedicated cost critic $Q_c(s, a)$, and the Lagrange multiplier $\lambda_c$, updating $\lambda_c$ via dual ascent against the target collisions-per-kilometer budget.**
4. **Scale-Invariant Running-Percentile Gate and Cost-Aware Truncation:** Literature on adaptive model rollouts (M2AC, MACURA) highlights that raw ensemble variance changes by orders of magnitude across tasks and training stages, making static hand-tuned thresholds brittle. Furthermore, because collisions are rare in safe driving distributions, pre-collision states naturally exhibit elevated epistemic uncertainty; a naive uncertainty gate risks discarding the very states needed to learn collision avoidance. **We plan to (a) normalize ensemble disagreement by its running median and define the gate via an adaptive running percentile rather than a fixed scalar cutoff, and (b) introduce a *cost-aware uncertainty gate* (augmented with dedicated ensemble cost and termination heads) that retains high-uncertainty transitions when predicted collision probability is high—recording them as terminal cost transitions rather than discarding them.**
5. **Multi-Step Imagined Targets, Custom SAC Loop, and Expanded Ablations:** Standard Stable-Baselines3 (SB3) SAC does not natively support dual real/imagined replay buffers, variable-horizon imagined backups, or Lagrangian dual updates. **We plan to implement a clean, modular PyTorch SAC-Lagrangian pipeline from scratch (using SB3 only as a sanity check for model-free SAC), adopt Dreamer-SAC's *n*-step temporal-difference targets on freshly generated imagined rollouts alongside 1-step targets on off-policy real data, and expand our evaluation from a single MBPO comparison to a full ablation matrix (BC, model-free SAC, SAC-Lagrangian, horizon-swept MBPO, penalty-only, gate-only, cost-unaware gate + penalty, and full GATE-SAC) across multiple random seeds on unseen held-out driving maps.**

## 2. Related Work

GATE-SAC sits at the intersection of six research areas: (2.1) off-policy maximum-entropy actor-critic methods, (2.2) model-based RL and learned world models, (2.3) uncertainty-aware rollout truncation, (2.4) pessimism under model uncertainty, (2.5) safe RL via constrained MDPs, and (2.6) imitation learning and imitation-to-RL fine-tuning, evaluated within (2.7) autonomous driving simulation benchmarks. Below, we examine the technical mechanisms of the most closely related works and contrast each with our planned project.

### 2.1 Off-Policy Maximum-Entropy Actor-Critic Foundations

**Soft Actor-Critic (Haarnoja et al., 2018a; Haarnoja et al., 2018b).** Standard RL seeks a policy maximizing expected discounted return $\sum_t \mathbb{E}[\gamma^t r(s_t, a_t)]$. Soft Actor-Critic (Haarnoja et al., 2018a) instead optimizes a *maximum-entropy* objective,
$$J(\pi) = \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \mathbb{E}_{(s_t, a_t) \sim \rho_\pi} \Big[ \gamma^t \big( r(s_t, a_t) + \alpha \, \mathcal{H}(\pi(\cdot \mid s_t)) \big) \Big],$$
where $\mathcal{H}(\pi(\cdot \mid s)) = \mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi}[-\log \pi(a \mid s)]$ rewards stochasticity weighted by temperature $\alpha > 0$. Technically, SAC rests on *soft policy iteration*, alternating between (i) *soft policy evaluation*, which iterates the soft Bellman operator $\mathcal{T}^\pi Q(s, a) = r(s, a) + \gamma \, \mathbb{E}_{s' \sim P}[V(s')]$ with soft state value $V(s) = \mathbb{E}_{a' \sim \pi}[Q(s, a') - \alpha \log \pi(a' \mid s)]$, and (ii) *soft policy improvement*, which projects the policy toward the exponentiated Q-function in Kullback–Leibler divergence, $\min_{\pi'} D_{\text{KL}}\big(\pi'(\cdot \mid s) \,\|\, \exp(Q^\pi(s, \cdot)/\alpha)/Z^\pi(s)\big)$. For continuous action spaces bounded in $[-1, 1]^d$, SAC parameterizes $\pi_\phi(a \mid s)$ as a diagonal Gaussian squashed through $\tanh$, applying the reparameterization trick $a = \tanh(\mu_\phi(s) + \sigma_\phi(s) \odot \epsilon)$ with $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$ and exact Jacobian log-determinant correction $\log \pi(a \mid s) = \log \mathcal{N}(u; \mu_\phi, \sigma_\phi^2) - \sum_j \log(1 - \tanh^2(u_j))$ so low-variance pathwise gradients flow directly from the critic into $\phi$. In their follow-up work, Haarnoja et al. (2018b) eliminate the separate state-value network and reformulate entropy maximization as a constrained optimization problem requiring $\mathbb{E}[-\log \pi(a \mid s)] \ge \bar{\mathcal{H}}$ (typically $\bar{\mathcal{H}} = -\dim(\mathcal{A})$), automatically tuning $\alpha$ via dual gradient descent on $J(\alpha) = \mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi}[-\alpha \log \pi(a \mid s) - \alpha \bar{\mathcal{H}}]$.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* We adopt the two-critic, automatic-temperature SAC formulation of Haarnoja et al. (2018b) as our core off-policy optimizer because off-policy TD backups allow arbitrary mixtures of expert demonstrations, real simulator transitions, and synthetic world-model rollouts, while squashed Gaussian policies yield smooth continuous steering and throttle control. Unlike standard SAC—which is purely model-free, safety-unconstrained, and trained from scratch—our planned framework augments SAC with an ensemble world model, per-sample *n*-step bootstrap discounts for gated imagined rollouts, an annealed BC regularizer, and a Lagrangian cost critic. Automatic temperature tuning is particularly important in our planned setup because an imitation-initialized policy begins with much lower entropy than a random policy.

**Clipped Double-Q Learning and TD3 (Fujimoto et al., 2018).** Fujimoto et al. demonstrate that function approximation noise in continuous actor-critic methods induces systematic value *overestimation*: because the actor updates $\phi$ along $\nabla_a Q_\theta(s, a)$, it actively seeks out actions where approximation error in $Q_\theta$ is positive, and Bellman bootstrapping compounds these upward errors across updates. Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient (TD3) counters this via three mechanisms: (i) *clipped double-Q learning*, training two independent critics $Q_{\theta_1}, Q_{\theta_2}$ and computing targets using the pessimistic minimum $y = r + \gamma \min_{i=1,2} Q_{\bar{\theta}_i}(s', \tilde{a}')$ over Polyak-averaged target networks $\bar{\theta}_i$; (ii) *target policy smoothing*, injecting clipped Gaussian noise into $\tilde{a}'$ to smooth narrow value spikes; and (iii) *delayed policy updates*.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* GATE-SAC retains clipped double-Q learning and Polyak target networks (via SAC) for its task-reward critics. Whereas TD3 uses deterministic policies and addresses only *critic* approximation error in model-free settings, MBRL introduces a second, often more severe source of optimism: *world-model* approximation error, where the actor exploits hallucinated transitions or rewards. Our planned design pairs critic-level pessimism ($\min_{i=1,2} Q_i$) with model-level pessimism (uncertainty gating and MOPO reward penalization).

### 2.2 Model-Based RL, Learned Ensembles, and Latent World Models

**Dyna Architecture (Sutton, 1990).** Sutton's foundational Dyna framework unifies direct RL and model-based planning in a single loop. Real interactions $(s, a, r, s')$ update both the value/policy parameters and a learned environment model $\hat{P}, \hat{R}$; between real steps, the agent samples previously visited states, queries the model to generate hypothetical transitions, and applies the exact same RL update rule to synthetic experience as to real experience.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* GATE-SAC follows the Dyna paradigm—using the learned world model strictly as a fast *experience generator* for off-policy value and policy updates rather than differentiating directly through multi-step dynamics or running online trajectory optimization at execution time. Unlike classical tabular Dyna-Q, which assumes single-step queries from a uniform model without uncertainty awareness or safety constraints, GATE-SAC operates in high-dimensional continuous driving state spaces using branched multi-step ensemble rollouts filtered by epistemic uncertainty.

**Deep Ensembles (Lakshminarayanan et al., 2017) and PETS (Chua et al., 2018).** Accurately separating what a dynamics model *does not know* (*epistemic* uncertainty, reducible with data) from inherent environmental stochasticity (*aleatoric* uncertainty) is central to reliable MBRL. Lakshminarayanan et al. (2017) show that training an ensemble of $E$ neural networks independently from distinct random initializations using a proper scoring rule—specifically Gaussian negative log-likelihood (NLL),
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(\psi_i) = \frac{1}{2}\big(\mu_{\psi_i}(x) - y\big)^\top \Sigma_{\psi_i}^{-1}(x)\big(\mu_{\psi_i}(x) - y\big) + \frac{1}{2}\log\det \Sigma_{\psi_i}(x),$$
produces well-calibrated predictive distributions whose inter-member variance increases sharply on out-of-distribution inputs. Probabilistic Ensembles with Trajectory Sampling (PETS; Chua et al., 2018) brings this architecture to continuous-control MBRL: each of $E$ bootstrapped neural networks predicts a diagonal Gaussian over state residuals $\Delta s = s_{t+1} - s_t$ (stabilized by learnable soft bounds on $\log \sigma^2$), and candidate action sequences are evaluated online via particle propagation (Trajectory Sampling) inside Model Predictive Control (MPC) optimized with the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM).  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* We adopt the PETS probabilistic bootstrap ensemble architecture—predicting normalized state deltas $\Delta s$ and rewards under Gaussian NLL—and rely on Lakshminarayanan et al.'s epistemic disagreement (variance across ensemble members' predicted next-state means) as our core uncertainty signal $u(s, a)$. However, PETS has no explicit policy network and relies on online CEM-MPC planning at every environment step, which is computationally expensive at real-time driving rates and struggles with long-horizon credit assignment. GATE-SAC amortizes decision-making into a fast SAC actor trained offline/online on ensemble rollouts, and extends the PETS network architecture with binary classification heads for safety cost and episode termination.

**MVE (Feinberg et al., 2018) and STEVE (Buckman et al., 2018).** Model-Based Value Expansion (MVE; Feinberg et al., 2018) uses a learned deterministic dynamics model to unroll $H$ imagined steps from real replay transitions solely to construct lower-bias multi-step TD targets $y_t^{(H)} = \sum_{k=0}^{H-1} \gamma^k \hat{r}_{t+k} + \gamma^H Q_{\bar{\theta}}(\hat{s}_{t+H}, \pi(\hat{s}_{t+H}))$, updating the critic only at the real starting state. Because any fixed $H$ degrades when the model is inaccurate, Stochastic Ensemble Value Expansion (STEVE; Buckman et al., 2018) maintains ensembles of both the dynamics model and the Q-function, computes candidate targets $y_t^{(h)}$ for every horizon $h \in \{0, \dots, H\}$, estimates the empirical variance $\text{Var}[y_t^{(h)}]$ across ensemble rollouts, and forms an inverse-variance-weighted average target $\sum_h w_h \, \mathbb{E}[y_t^{(h)}]$ with $w_h \propto 1/\text{Var}[y_t^{(h)}]$.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* STEVE pioneered the principle of *per-transition, uncertainty-adaptive rollout horizons*, smoothly falling back to shorter horizons when multi-step variance spikes. However, STEVE uses imagined rollouts *only* to form scalar critic targets for real replay states—discarding all intermediate imagined states $(\hat{s}_{t+1}, \dots, \hat{s}_{t+H-1})$ as training inputs for the actor and critic—and requires evaluating every horizon up to $H$ across both model and critic ensembles. GATE-SAC instead truncates rollouts *during data generation* and trains both the actor and critics directly on the surviving imagined transitions using variable-length *n*-step backups, while adding safety constraints that STEVE lacks.

**MBPO: When to Trust Your Model (Janner et al., 2019).** Janner et al. provide the theoretical and practical foundation for modern Dyna-style deep RL. Theoretically, they prove a monotonic policy improvement bound showing that if full-length rollouts are generated under a learned model with generalization error $\epsilon_m$ under policy shift $\epsilon_\pi$, return discrepancy scales poorly as $\mathcal{O}(\epsilon_m / (1 - \gamma)^2)$; however, if *short $k$-step rollouts branch from states drawn from the real replay distribution* ( collected under past policies), compounding model error is bounded over $k$ steps rather than $1/(1-\gamma)$, yielding a non-trivial optimal horizon $k^* > 0$. Algorithmically, MBPO trains a PETS-style probabilistic MLP ensemble, periodically samples batches of real states from $\mathcal{D}_{\text{env}}$, rolls out the current SAC policy for a fixed (or linearly scheduled) short horizon $k$ by randomly selecting an elite ensemble member at each step, stores transitions in $\mathcal{D}_{\text{model}}$, and performs a high ratio of SAC gradient updates per environment step ($G \in [20, 40]$) on mini-batches mostly drawn from $\mathcal{D}_{\text{model}}$ (e.g., 95% imagined, 5% real).  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* MBPO is our architectural backbone and primary baseline. We preserve MBPO's short branched rollouts from real replay states, random-member transition sampling, high update-to-data (UTD) ratio, and hybrid real/imagined mini-batches. Crucially, however, MBPO applies a *uniform, uncertainty-blind horizon $k$* to every starting state: if $k$ is set conservatively (e.g., $k=1$), sample efficiency suffers on well-modeled road segments; if $k$ is larger (e.g., $k=5$ or $10$), branches entering unfamiliar traffic interactions hallucinate and corrupt the critic. MBPO also includes no pessimism penalty, no safety constraint, and no imitation prior. GATE-SAC replaces MBPO's uniform horizon with per-branch cost-aware uncertainty gating, adds MOPO reward pessimism, enforces a Lagrangian collision budget, and initializes from BC.

**PlaNet, Dreamer, and Dreamer-SAC (Hafner et al., 2019; Hafner et al., 2020; Li et al., 2026).** For high-dimensional visual inputs, PlaNet (Hafner et al., 2019) introduced the *Recurrent State-Space Model* (RSSM), decomposing latent dynamics into a deterministic recurrent state $h_t = f_\theta(h_{t-1}, z_{t-1}, a_{t-1})$ and a stochastic state $z_t \sim p_\theta(z_t \mid h_t)$ trained via a variational evidence lower bound (ELBO) reconstructing observations and rewards. Dreamer (Hafner et al., 2020) trains an actor and value network purely on-policy inside 15-step latent RSSM imaginations by backpropagating analytic value gradients of $\lambda$-returns directly through the learned dynamics. Recently, **Dreamer-SAC** (Li et al., 2026) adapted latent world modeling to autonomous driving by coupling an RSSM (over camera and LiDAR inputs) with **off-policy SAC** rather than Dreamer's on-policy actor-critic. Dreamer-SAC branches short latent rollouts from real encoded states, discards and regenerates the imagined buffer each round to avoid stale dynamics, mixes real and imagined batches, and introduces *n*-step TD backups specifically on imagined rollouts,
$$y_t^{\text{img}} = \sum_{k=0}^{N-1} \gamma^k \hat{r}_{t+k} + \gamma^N \Big( \min_{i=1,2} Q_{\bar{\theta}_i}(\hat{s}_{t+N}, a'_{t+N}) - \alpha \log \pi(a'_{t+N} \mid \hat{s}_{t+N}) \Big),$$
while retaining 1-step TD targets on off-policy real transitions. Empirically, Li et al. uncover a pronounced *inverted-U* relationship between fixed horizon $H$ and driving return (peaking at $H=5$ before degrading from accumulated model bias) and explicitly identify *uncertainty-adaptive rollout horizons* as a key direction for future work.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* Dreamer-SAC directly motivates several aspects of GATE-SAC: we adopt its hybrid real/imagined SAC update, per-round imagined buffer refresh, and *n*-step imagined TD backups (where $N$ naturally adapts to the surviving rollout length after gating), while pursuing an RSSM vision pipeline as our stretch goal. Our core GATE-SAC design differs from Dreamer-SAC in four ways: (i) we operate in vector state space with an *ensemble* of four MLPs rather than a single RSSM, giving us explicit epistemic disagreement $u(s, a)$; (ii) we replace Dreamer-SAC's fixed horizon $H=5$ with per-branch cost-aware uncertainty gating and MOPO reward pessimism; (iii) we enforce hard safety budgets via a Lagrangian cost critic rather than scalarized multi-objective rewards; and (iv) we fine-tune an imitation-pretrained policy on failure scenarios rather than learning from scratch.

### 2.3 Uncertainty-Aware Rollout Truncation

**M2AC: Masked Model-based Actor-Critic (Pan et al., 2020).** Pan et al. establish theoretically that MBRL policy return error depends only on the local model error at *accepted* synthetic state-action pairs, motivating selective masking of imagined transitions. During an $H$-step branched rollout in a probabilistic ensemble, M2AC computes an epistemic uncertainty score for each transition using a one-versus-rest Kullback–Leibler divergence between one ensemble member's Gaussian prediction and the mixture of the remaining $E-1$ members. Rather than comparing against a fixed scalar threshold, M2AC ranks all $B$ parallel rollouts at step $h$ and keeps only the lowest-uncertainty fraction $w_h$ (a *rank-based percentile mask*), halting masked branches and penalizing surviving rewards by $-\alpha u(s, a)$.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* M2AC is closely aligned with our planned combination of rank/percentile gating and reward penalization: rank-based masking makes truncation invariant to the raw numerical scale of ensemble variance as the world model improves over training. However, M2AC was designed for unconstrained MuJoCo locomotion and treats *every* high-uncertainty transition identically—by discarding it. In safety-critical driving, rare near-collision states lie in sparse regions of the replay buffer and therefore trigger high model disagreement; M2AC's blind masking discards these transitions, starving the agent of collision-avoidance signal. GATE-SAC introduces a *cost-aware* gate to preserve predicted high-cost terminals alongside a Constrained MDP safety layer and imitation pretraining.

**Uncertainty-Aware MBRL for Autonomous Driving (Wu et al., 2021).** Wu et al. bring ensemble-based adaptive truncation specifically to autonomous driving. They train an action-conditioned ensemble of dynamics networks to estimate epistemic prediction variance across ensemble heads during virtual policy rollouts and truncate an imagined trajectory whenever variance exceeds a predefined threshold, feeding only pre-truncation transitions into off-policy actor-critic updates. They demonstrate improved sample efficiency and robustness over fixed-horizon MBPO and model-free baselines across dynamic driving scenarios.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* Wu et al. (2021) serve as our primary domain precedent for adaptive rollout truncation in autonomous driving. GATE-SAC advances beyond their framework in four ways: (i) we replace a static hand-tuned variance cutoff with a normalized running-percentile threshold so truncation adapts automatically as world-model accuracy evolves; (ii) we pair truncation with MOPO pessimistic reward shaping on surviving steps; (iii) we explicitly address truncation behavior near rare collision events via our cost-aware gate; and (iv) we integrate Lagrangian safety constraints and imitation-learning initialization.

**MACURA (Frauenknecht et al., 2024).** Model-Based Actor-Critic with Uncertainty-Aware Rollout Adaption (MACURA) addresses threshold calibration in per-branch rollout truncation. MACURA measures epistemic disagreement across a probabilistic ensemble via the geometric Jensen–Shannon divergence between member Gaussians and dynamically calibrates its truncation threshold at each rollout round relative to the empirical quantile (e.g., 95th percentile) of uncertainty measured at the *first* rollout step ($h=1$, anchored on real replay states), scaled by a tunable factor $\xi$. Rollouts proceed up to $H_{\max}$ and terminate individually as soon as branch uncertainty crosses this self-calibrated bound.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* MACURA reinforces the importance of *online, distribution-relative threshold calibration* over fixed thresholds or manual horizon schedules. Similar to MACURA, GATE-SAC computes its truncation threshold dynamically from running empirical statistics of ensemble disagreement. Unlike MACURA—which operates on unconstrained robotics benchmarks without reward pessimism, imitation priors, or safety budgets, and unconditionally drops all supra-threshold steps—GATE-SAC incorporates predicted safety cost into the truncation rule and enforces a per-kilometer collision constraint.

### 2.4 Pessimism Under Model Uncertainty

**MOPO: Model-based Offline Policy Optimization (Yu et al., 2020).** In offline RL, an agent learns from a static dataset without environment interaction, making world-model exploitation catastrophic because errors in out-of-distribution regions are never corrected by real feedback. Yu et al. prove that if an uncertainty quantifier $u(s, a)$ upper-bounds the expected value error of a learned dynamics model $\hat{P}$, i.e., $\big|\mathbb{E}_{s'\sim \hat{P}}[V^*(s')] - \mathbb{E}_{s'\sim P}[V^*(s')]\big| \le \gamma \, u(s, a)$ up to scaling, then optimizing a policy inside the *uncertainty-penalized MDP* with modified reward
$$\tilde{r}(s, a) = \hat{r}(s, a) - \lambda \, u(s, a)$$
maximizes a rigorous lower bound on true environment return. Practically, MOPO runs fixed-horizon MBPO over a probabilistic ensemble and sets $u(s, a) = \max_{i=1..E} \lVert \Sigma_{\psi_i}(s, a) \rVert_F$.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* GATE-SAC directly adopts MOPO's pessimistic reward transformation $\tilde{r} = \hat{r} - \lambda \tilde{u}$ to soften the boundary below the truncation gate, discouraging the SAC actor from exploiting moderate model errors on surviving imagined steps. Our planned approach differs from MOPO in three respects: (i) MOPO is purely *offline* with fixed rollout horizons, whereas GATE-SAC operates in *online* closed-loop fine-tuning with adaptive horizon gating; (ii) MOPO uses maximum single-member covariance norm (primarily capturing aleatoric noise), whereas we use normalized *inter-member mean disagreement* to target epistemic model ignorance; and (iii) MOPO conflates epistemic caution with task reward, whereas GATE-SAC separates epistemic model pessimism ($\lambda \tilde{u}$) from physical safety constraints ($\lambda_c Q_c$).

**MOReL: Model-Based Offline RL (Kidambi et al., 2020).** Rather than subtracting a continuous uncertainty penalty at every step, MOReL constructs an explicit *Pessimistic MDP* (P-MDP) using an Unknown State-Action Detector (USAD) defined by maximum pairwise ensemble prediction discrepancy $\max_{i,j} \lVert f_{\psi_i}(s, a) - f_{\psi_j}(s, a) \rVert_2 > \tau$. Any transition triggering the USAD is redirected to an absorbing `HALT` state that immediately terminates the imagined episode with a large negative reward $-\kappa$; transitions below $\tau$ proceed unmodified. Kidambi et al. prove near-optimality bounds for policies trained in the P-MDP via natural policy gradient.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* MOReL's absorbing `HALT` transition provides an insightful contrast to MBPO/MACURA truncation: dropping an uncertain transition simply withholds data, whereas transitioning to a penalized terminal actively teaches the policy *never to enter* that state-action region. In online fine-tuning, applying MOReL's `HALT` penalty to *all* uncertain states is overly conservative, as it prevents exploration in benign novel road geometries. Our planned **cost-aware gate** selectively applies a MOReL-like terminal transition *only* when both epistemic uncertainty and predicted collision probability are high (assigning terminal safety cost $c=1$ priced by $\lambda_c$), while silently truncating benign high-uncertainty rollouts.

### 2.5 Safe Reinforcement Learning and Constrained MDPs

**Constrained MDPs (Altman, 1999), CPO (Achiam et al., 2017), Safety Gym (Ray et al., 2019), and PID-Lagrangian (Stooke et al., 2020).** Altman (1999) formalized the **Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP)**, augmenting an MDP with a non-negative cost function $c(s, a)$ and safety threshold $d$:
$$\max_{\pi} \; J_R(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \gamma^t r(s_t, a_t)\right] \quad \text{s.t.} \quad J_C(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_\pi\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \gamma^t c(s_t, a_t)\right] \le d.$$
By Lagrangian duality, constrained optimization can be cast as the saddle-point problem $\min_{\lambda_c \ge 0} \max_\pi \, J_R(\pi) - \lambda_c \big(J_C(\pi) - d\big)$. Constrained Policy Optimization (CPO; Achiam et al., 2017) solves CMDPs on-policy without dual variables by linearizing both the reward objective and cost constraint around the current policy inside a second-order Fisher-information KL trust region, guaranteeing monotonic return improvement and approximate per-iteration constraint satisfaction. However, in the comprehensive *Safety Gym* benchmark, Ray et al. (2019) show that CPO's Taylor approximations and conjugate-gradient steps often underperform simpler primal-dual **Lagrangian methods** that alternate policy optimization with projected dual gradient ascent $\lambda_c \leftarrow \max(0, \lambda_c + \eta (\hat{J}_C - d))$. Addressing the tendency of standard dual ascent (a pure integral controller) to oscillate around the budget $d$, Stooke et al. (2020) introduce **PID-Lagrangian** control, adding proportional ($K_P \max(0, \hat{J}_C - d)$) and derivative ($K_D (\hat{J}_C^{(k)} - \hat{J}_C^{(k-1)})$) damping terms to stabilize constraint satisfaction during training.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* Because CPO requires on-policy trajectory advantage estimates that are incompatible with high-UTD off-policy replay buffers and synthetic model rollouts, GATE-SAC adopts the primal-dual CMDP formulation of Altman (1999) and Ray et al. (2019), defining $d$ directly in terms of real-world driving safety (collisions and road departures per kilometer) with PID-Lagrangian damping (Stooke et al., 2020) available if dual updates oscillate.

**SAC-Lagrangian (Ha et al., 2020), WCSAC (Yang et al., 2021), and SafeRL-Kit (Zhang et al., 2022).** Ha et al. (2020) extend off-policy SAC to CMDPs for real-world robot locomotion by introducing a separate neural safety critic $Q_c(s, a)$ trained via standard Bellman backups on safety costs $y_c = c + \gamma(1-d) Q_{\bar{c}}(s', a')$, modifying the SAC actor loss to
$$L_\pi(\phi) = \mathbb{E}_{s \sim \mathcal{D},\, a \sim \pi_\phi} \Big[ \alpha \log \pi_\phi(a \mid s) - \min_{i=1,2} Q_{\theta_i}(s, a) + \lambda_c \, Q_c(s, a) \Big].$$
Worst-Case SAC (WCSAC; Yang et al., 2021) further replaces the expectation-based cost critic with a distributional Gaussian cost critic predicting both mean and variance of cumulative cost, constraining the Conditional Value-at-Risk ($\text{CVaR}$) to capture tail-risk collisions. In autonomous driving, **SafeRL-Kit** (Zhang et al., 2022) systematically benchmarks off-policy safe RL algorithms—including SAC-Lagrangian, Safety Layer, Recovery RL, Feasible Actor-Critic, and Exact Penalty Optimization—demonstrating that two-critic off-policy Lagrangian architectures achieve strong safety-efficiency trade-offs on collision-avoidance tasks.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* Our constrained learner follows the two-critic SAC-Lagrangian architecture of Ha et al. (2020) and SafeRL-Kit (Zhang et al., 2022). All three of these frameworks, however, are strictly *model-free*, requiring hundreds of thousands of real environment interactions and real safety violations to train $Q_c$ and $\pi$. GATE-SAC embeds SAC-Lagrangian inside an uncertainty-gated world-model imagination loop initialized from imitation learning.

**Safe Model-Based RL: SMBPO (Thomas et al., 2021), LAMBDA (As et al., 2022), and EGPO (Peng et al., 2021).** Several works combine world models or experts with safety. **Safe MBPO** (SMBPO; Thomas et al., 2021) assumes a known analytical indicator for terminal unsafe states and uses short MBPO rollouts as a lookahead shield: whenever an imagined trajectory enters an unsafe state, the reward is overwritten with a fixed penalty $-C$ guaranteed (above a theoretical threshold on $C$) to make unsafe trajectories suboptimal. **LAMBDA** (As et al., 2022) places a Bayesian SWAG posterior over a pixel-based RSSM, rolling out multiple posterior parameter samples in imagination to construct an *optimistic* upper bound on task return and a *pessimistic* upper confidence bound on safety cost inside an augmented Lagrangian update. From an expert-guided model-free angle, **EGPO** (Peng et al., 2021) embeds a pretrained PPO expert inside `SafeMetaDriveEnv` as an online guardian that intervenes whenever the RL agent proposes risky actions, constraining intervention frequency via a Lagrangian multiplier.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* SMBPO inspires our treatment of imagined crashes as penalized terminal transitions, but SMBPO assumes ground-truth safety termination is known *a priori*, relies on a fixed scalar reward penalty rather than a CMDP cost critic, and performs no uncertainty gating. LAMBDA shares our combination of epistemic world-model uncertainty and Lagrangian constraints, yet operates on-policy inside a Bayesian RSSM without rollout truncation or imitation initialization. Finally, unlike EGPO—which requires querying an interactive expert guardian online at every training step—GATE-SAC uses demonstrations only offline for initial Behavior Cloning and replay seeding, relying on uncertainty-gated imagination and Lagrangian cost critics during closed-loop RL.

### 2.6 Imitation Learning and Imitation-to-RL Fine-Tuning

**Behavior Cloning, Covariate Shift, and Interactive/Noise-Injected IL: ALVINN (Pomerleau, 1989), DAgger (Ross et al., 2011), and CIL (Codevilla et al., 2018).** End-to-end imitation driving dates to **ALVINN** (Pomerleau, 1989), which trained a shallow MLP to map camera and laser range-finder inputs to steering commands via supervised regression $\min_\phi \mathbb{E}_{(s, a^*) \sim \mathcal{D}_{\text{exp}}} \lVert \pi_\phi(s) - a^* \rVert^2$. Pomerleau observed that because human demonstrations remain near lane center, a pure Behavior Cloning policy never observes recovery maneuvers from off-center drift. **DAgger** (*Dataset Aggregation*; Ross et al., 2011) formalized this *covariate shift* failure mode: if a cloned policy makes bounded per-step error $\epsilon$ on $\rho_{\pi^*}$, small deviations push the vehicle into unvisited states where errors compound quadratically as $\mathcal{O}(T^2 \epsilon)$ over horizon $T$. DAgger reduces error to $\mathcal{O}(T \epsilon)$ via iterative online expert relabeling of learner-visited states. For urban autonomous driving without an online relabeler, **Conditional Imitation Learning** (CIL; Codevilla et al., 2018) conditions a branched perception-control network on high-level route commands and injects triangular noise perturbations into data-collection steering while recording the expert's corrective actions.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* Covariate shift ($\mathcal{O}(T^2 \epsilon)$ compounding error) is the exact failure mode our project seeks to repair. Unlike DAgger, which assumes an interactive oracle expert can label arbitrary states online during deployment/training, GATE-SAC assumes only a *fixed offline demonstration dataset* for pretraining Behavior Cloning, using closed-loop safe MBRL to learn recovery behaviors autonomously in scenarios where BC fails.

**Demonstration-Guided RL and Offline-to-Online Fine-Tuning: DDPGfD (Večerík et al., 2017), DAPG (Rajeswaran et al., 2018), AWAC (Nair et al., 2020), and BC-SAC (Lu et al., 2023).** Bridging imitation pretraining into RL without catastrophic forgetting is a well-studied challenge. **DDPG from Demonstrations** (DDPGfD; Večerík et al., 2017) retains expert transitions permanently inside an off-policy replay buffer and combines 1-step and *n*-step TD backups so critics rapidly propagate value along demonstrated trajectories. **Demonstration-Augmented Policy Gradient** (DAPG; Rajeswaran et al., 2018) initializes an on-policy actor via BC and augments the policy gradient with an exponentially decaying behavior-cloning log-likelihood weight $\lambda_0 \lambda_1^k$ so the policy stays near expert behavior early in training before surpassing the demonstrator. In off-policy actor-critic settings, **AWAC** (*Advantage Weighted Actor-Critic*; Nair et al., 2020) proves why naive fine-tuning collapses: randomly initialized or offline-trained critics produce wildly inaccurate Q-gradients on out-of-distribution actions, immediately destroying a pretrained actor's weights before the critic converges. AWAC avoids explicit Q-gradient maximization via an implicit KL-constrained weighted maximum-likelihood update. Directly in autonomous driving at Waymo scale, **Lu et al. (2023)** (*"Imitation Is Not Enough"*, **BC-SAC**) show that pure imitation policies fail on rare safety-critical tail scenarios; they resolve this by jointly optimizing a hybrid actor objective combining SAC's off-policy RL loss with a weighted BC loss over human driving logs in closed-loop simulation, substantially reducing collisions on challenging scenarios while preserving nominal driving quality.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* BC-SAC (Lu et al., 2023) directly validates our problem formulation—using off-policy SAC to cure tail-scenario failures of an imitation driving policy—while DDPGfD, DAPG, and AWAC motivate our planned initialization safeguards (seeding the replay buffer with demonstrations, warming up the critics with the actor frozen, and adding an annealed BC regularizer to the SAC actor loss). Critically, however, BC-SAC, AWAC, DAPG, and DDPGfD are all **model-free** and handle safety only via fixed scalar reward shaping (or not at all). GATE-SAC brings sample-efficient, uncertainty-gated world-model imagination and formal CMDP safety bounds to the IL-to-RL driving paradigm.

### 2.7 Autonomous Driving Benchmarks, Datasets, and Open Challenges

**MetaDrive (Li et al., 2023a), ScenarioNet (Li et al., 2023b), Waymo Open Motion Dataset (Ettinger et al., 2021), and DRL Driving Surveys (Kiran et al., 2022).** Evaluating generalization and safety in RL driving requires diverse road topologies and interactive traffic. **MetaDrive** (Li et al., 2023a) composes unlimited procedural road networks from parameterized road blocks (curves, ramps, intersections, roundabouts) alongside reactive IDM traffic and static hazards (`SafeMetaDriveEnv`), demonstrating that training across diverse procedural seeds enables zero-shot generalization to unseen test maps. **ScenarioNet** (Li et al., 2023b) bridges procedural simulation and real-world logs by converting datasets such as the **Waymo Open Motion Dataset** (WOMD; Ettinger et al., 2021)—which provides over 100,000 20-second 10 Hz multi-agent trajectory scenes and HD maps—into replayable MetaDrive environments. Finally, **Kiran et al. (2022)** survey deep RL for autonomous driving and highlight four primary barriers to deployment: low sample efficiency, lack of formal safety guarantees, brittle manual reward engineering, and covariate shift in pure imitation.  
*Comparison to GATE-SAC:* MetaDrive (`SafeMetaDriveEnv`) provides our closed-loop training and unseen-seed evaluation testbed, while WOMD and ScenarioNet motivate our real-world evaluation stretch goal (noting that WOMD provides kinematic trajectories rather than ground-truth pedal/steering actuations). Conceptually, GATE-SAC is designed to address all four open challenges identified by Kiran et al. (2022) within a single unified architecture.

**Research Gap Addressed by GATE-SAC.** Existing uncertainty-truncated MBRL algorithms (M2AC, Wu et al., MACURA) were developed without formal safety constraints and rely on the assumption that high model disagreement always warrants discarding synthetic transitions. Conversely, safe RL and imitation-fine-tuning methods in autonomous driving (SafeRL-Kit, EGPO, BC-SAC) remain predominantly model-free, while recent driving world models (Dreamer-SAC) use fixed rollout horizons without epistemic gating or CMDP safety bounds. In safety-critical autonomous driving, collision and near-collision states lie in the sparse tail of the experience distribution and therefore naturally induce high epistemic disagreement across ensemble members—creating a fundamental tension between *filtering out model hallucinations* and *preserving safety-critical learning signals*. GATE-SAC is designed specifically to resolve this tension by uniting imitation fine-tuning, scale-invariant adaptive rollout gating, cost-aware retention of predicted hazards, MOPO reward pessimism, and Lagrangian safety constraints in a single sample-efficient framework.
